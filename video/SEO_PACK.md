## What changed and why

| Tactic | What it means here |
|---|---|
| Name the question in the title | Each title now starts with the words people type into YouTube and Google ("What is embodied carbon", "LEED vs EDGE"). The witty hook moves later in the title. |
| Answer in the first line | The first one or two lines of each description answer the question outright. Search snippets and AI answers read that part first. |
| Link the answers together | Each description points to the episode that answers the viewer's next question. |
| Chapters | **Shorts don't show chapters.** So there's also a full-series video (below), where chapters do work and can appear in Google results. |
| Drop `#shorts` from titles | YouTube detects vertical videos under 3 minutes as Shorts by itself. Keep `#shorts` in the description hashtags only, which frees title space for keywords. |

**Title:** What Is Embodied Carbon? The CO₂ Hidden in Concrete and Steel

**Opening lines (replace the first paragraph):**
Embodied carbon is the CO₂ released making, transporting and building with materials like cement and steel, before anyone moves in. Cement and steel alone produce around 15% of global CO₂, roughly seven times all of aviation.

## Episode 2: Why "Net Zero" Can Mislead You

**Title:** Is a "Net Zero" Building Really Net Zero? Operational vs Embodied Carbon

**Opening lines:**
Most "net zero" building claims cover operational carbon, the energy used every day. They often leave out embodied carbon, which is already emitted by the time the building opens. Ask which one a claim covers, ideally both as whole-life carbon (EN 15978).

▶ Next question: is it greener to keep an old building or build a new efficient one? Episode 3.

## Episode 3: Old Building vs LEED Platinum

**Title:** Renovate or Rebuild? Old Building vs LEED Platinum, Carbon Compared

**Opening lines:**
Reusing an existing building often beats demolishing and rebuilding on carbon. A new efficient building can take 10 to 80 years to make up for the carbon spent constructing it (Preservation Green Lab, 2011). It depends on the materials the retrofit uses.

## Episode 4: Your AC Is a Climate Supervillain

**Title:** Why Your AC's Refrigerant Can Be Worse Than Its Electricity (R-410A GWP)

**Opening lines:**
R-410A, a common AC refrigerant, traps about 2,088 times more heat than CO₂ (IPCC AR4, 100-year GWP). If a typical home system's full charge escapes, that's roughly a year of driving a car. Heat pumps deliver 3 to 4 units of heat per unit of electricity.

▶ Next question: which green building certification should a project choose? Episode 5.

## Episode 5: Green Certifications, Rated Like Street Food

**Title:** LEED vs EDGE vs IGBC vs GRIHA: Which Green Building Certification to Choose?

**Opening lines:**
LEED (USGBC) carries the most international recognition. EDGE (IFC) requires at least 20% savings in energy, water and embodied energy in materials. IGBC (part of CII) is widely used for Indian projects. GRIHA (TERI) is India's national rating system and is often required for government buildings. The comparisons in the video are opinion.

▶ Next question: what does a certification audit actually check on site? Episode 6.

## Episode 6: What a Green Building Audit Really Finds

**Title:** What Happens in a Green Building Audit? Site Verification Explained

**Opening lines:**
A green building site audit checks that what was built matches what was certified on paper: the glazing, insulation, fixtures and equipment in the design. The most common finding is a gap where something was swapped or value-engineered out during construction.

## Film: The Life of a Building

**Title:** Whole-Life Carbon Explained: Building LCA Stages A1 to D (EN 15978)

**Opening lines:**
A building's whole-life carbon is counted in stages under EN 15978: A1–A3 materials, A4–A5 construction, B1–B7 use, C1–C4 end of life, and D for reuse and recycling, reported separately. Many green claims cover only the use stage. A life cycle assessment (LCA) covers all of them.
