# Recording guide: your voice and your music

Your voice is the biggest upgrade these videos can get. It takes about 10 minutes per week: three takes of about a minute each.

## How to record (phone is fine)

1. **Room:** a small, soft room (bedroom with curtains, or a wardrobe of clothes behind you). Avoid bare walls, tiled rooms and fans. Switch off the AC and ceiling fan while recording.
2. **Phone:** your phone's own voice recorder app. Hold it about a hand's width (15–20 cm) from your mouth, slightly to the side so breaths don't hit it. Turn on flight mode so it doesn't buzz.
3. **Read once through out loud first.** Then record the whole video in **one take**.
4. **Pauses are the instructions for the editor.** Leave a **2-second pause between paragraphs** (where the sheet says so) and a natural breath between sentences. Don't rush them: the software uses the pauses to cut your take and time every caption and graphic to your words.
5. **Mistake?** Stop, pause 2 seconds, and start that **whole paragraph** again, then just tell me "paragraph 3 was re-read". Or simply record the whole take again.
6. **Voice:** speak like you're explaining it to a client across a table: a little slower than normal, smiling slightly, ending sentences down, not up.

## How to send

Attach each take in this chat (any format: m4a, mp3, wav, ogg/opus, amr), named as on its sheet (e.g. `g60_1.m4a`). I clean it (rumble filter, noise reduction, gentle compression), cut it at your pauses, re-time the visuals to your pacing, and send back the new video. You'll hear the result before you post.

## Music (optional, free)

1. In **YouTube Studio → Audio Library**, filter Genre "Ambient" or "Cinematic", Mood "Calm" or "Inspirational". Tracks marked "No attribution required" are easiest.
2. Pick 2–3 warm instrumental tracks, download them, and attach them here. (Audio Library tracks are free to use in your YouTube videos. Check the licence note on each track before using it on other platforms.)
3. I estimate each track's tempo, cut the scene changes to its beats, and duck it under your voice.

## Green Building In 60 Seconds · g60_1

Save the take as **g60_1.m4a** (any phone format works). About 137 words, aim for 53–60 seconds.

**1.** Same building.  /  Same AC.  /  So why is your flat so much hotter than your neighbour's?

*(pause 2 seconds)*

**2.** First, the sun.  /  West-facing flats take the harshest sun in the afternoon, right when the day is hottest.

*(pause 2 seconds)*

**3.** Second, the glass.  /  Big, unshaded windows let the heat straight in.  /  Clear glass with no shade is the worst combination.

*(pause 2 seconds)*

**4.** Third, the roof.  /  Top-floor flats sit under a slab that bakes all day and gives that heat back all night.

*(pause 2 seconds)*

**5.** Fourth, the walls.  /  Thin walls with no insulation pass the outside heat straight into your living room.

*(pause 2 seconds)*

**6.** The fixes are simpler than you think.  /  Shade the west windows from outside, add a white reflective roof coating, and run the ceiling fan before the AC.

*(pause 2 seconds)*

**7.** Green buildings solve this on paper, before anyone moves in.  /  So before you buy, ask which way the flat faces.

## Green Building In 60 Seconds · g60_3

Save the take as **g60_3.m4a** (any phone format works). About 120 words, aim for 46–52 seconds.

Say it like this: **EPD** = E-P-D; **EPDs** = E-P-Ds; **LEED** = leed (rhymes with 'need'); **EN 15804** = E-N fifteen-eight-oh-four; **LCA** = L-C-A

**1.** That bag of cement has a carbon footprint.  /  An EPD is how you read it.

*(pause 2 seconds)*

**2.** EPD stands for Environmental Product Declaration.  /  Think of it as a nutrition label for a building product.

*(pause 2 seconds)*

**3.** It reports the product's impacts, like its carbon, measured with a life cycle assessment.

*(pause 2 seconds)*

**4.** For construction products it follows EN 15804, and it's checked by an independent verifier before it's published.

*(pause 2 seconds)*

**5.** Most EPDs are valid for five years.  /  Always check the date, and the declared unit: per kilo, per square metre or per cubic metre.

*(pause 2 seconds)*

**6.** Why it matters: LEED rewards products with EPDs, and whole-building LCA tools use them as their data.

*(pause 2 seconds)*

**7.** So next time a supplier says low carbon, ask one thing.  /  Can I see the EPD?

*(stop)*
