# The Carbon You Can't See: YouTube Shorts upload pack

A six-part Shorts series. Upload them in order, one every 1–2 days, and add all six to a playlist named **"The Carbon You Can't See"** so YouTube shows the series link on each Short. Each video ends on a "Next episode" card pointing to the one after it.

How to use this pack:
- **Before you post Episode 6**, check that the first-person auditor narration is true for you. Short 6 says "I audit green buildings for a living."
- **Paste the shared credits block** (at the bottom) under every description.
- **Add an auto-captions track or upload the `.srt`** in each `out/series-epN/` folder. That helps viewers who watch muted, and helps search.
- **Every figure** below is in the fact-check log, `video/projects/FACT_CHECK.md`.

**Title:** Your AC Is Secretly a Climate Supervillain 😈 | The Carbon You Can't See Ep 4 #shorts

**Description:**
Your air conditioner's sneakiest climate impact isn't the electricity. It's the refrigerant. R-410A traps about 2,088 times more heat than CO₂, and one home system's full charge escaping is roughly a year of driving. Here's why heat pumps change the maths.

Episode 4 of The Carbon You Can't See.
◀ Previous: Old building vs LEED Platinum · ▶ Next: Green certifications, rated like street food

Sources:
• IEA, Tracking Buildings: building operations ≈ 30% of global final energy
• IPCC AR4 100-year GWP: R-410A = 2,088
• US EPA: a typical car emits ≈ 4.6 t CO₂ per year (a residential charge of 2.7–5.4 kg R-410A ≈ 5.6–11.3 t CO₂e if fully released)

Four green building certifications in about a minute: who made them, who they're for, and how to pick one. The comparisons are opinion, served street-food style.

Episode 5 of The Carbon You Can't See.
◀ Previous: Your AC is a climate supervillain · ▶ Next: What a green building audit really finds

Facts:
• LEED: U.S. Green Building Council; projects in 150+ countries and territories (USGBC)
• EDGE: created by IFC (World Bank Group); needs ≥20% savings in energy, water and embodied energy in materials versus a local baseline
• IGBC: Indian Green Building Council, part of CII
• GRIHA: developed by TERI and adopted as India's national rating system

## Posting checklist
1. Upload Episode 1. Add it to the playlist "The Carbon You Can't See".
2. Paste the title, description, tags and credits, then pin the comment.
3. Upload the matching `captions.srt` (Subtitles, then Upload file, then With timing).
4. Under Altered or synthetic content, the narrator is a synthetic voice that doesn't imitate any real person, and the visuals are clearly illustrated. YouTube's guidance generally doesn't require the label for that, but answering "Yes" costs nothing and avoids any doubt. The description says it's AI narration either way.
5. Repeat for Episodes 2–6, one every 1–2 days, and link each new episode in the pinned comment of the previous one.
